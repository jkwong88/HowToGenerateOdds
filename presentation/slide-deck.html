How to Generate a Final Score Probability Matrix

  • What: build the Final Score Probability Matrix, the Home × Away scoreline probabilities behind every market
  • Why: every market type is priced off this one matrix, so getting it right here keeps everything downstream correct
  • How (two steps): 1) Probability = Goal Count ÷ Number of Matches for Home/Away; 2) P(Home=h, Away=a) = P(Home=h) × P(Away=a), assuming independence

Opening Market

  • A fair market can be built for any line, but a bookmaker only ever quotes a few
  • Pick the candidate main line(s) whose fair probability sits closest to a 50/50 split
  • Then shave a pricing spread off the fair Malay odds to produce the quoted, overround-inclusive market

From Historical Probability to Poisson — intro & limitation
Page 14 / 17
Poisson

From Historical Probability to Poisson

  • Same rule as before: Probability = Count / Number of Matches (Historical / Empirical Probability)
  • The limitation: a goal count that never occurred shows 0%, not truly impossible, just unobserved in 10 matches
  • Poisson reframes the question, from "how often did this happen" to "given the expected rate, how likely is each goal count"
From Historical Probability to Poisson — formula & comparison

Poisson

The Poisson Formula & Comparison

  • Formula: P(X=k) = e-λ × λk / k! - fixed scoring rate, Home/Away modeled independently
  • Symbols: P(X=k) = probability of k goals; λ = mean scoring rate (total goals ÷ 10 matches, separately for Home/Away); k! = factorial

Back to the Matrix, and Poisson's Limits

  • Only the goal-probability estimate changes - the matrix → market → odds pipeline is identical to before
  • Worked example: P(Home=1) × P(Away=0) using Poisson-estimated probabilities, same independence formula as before
  • Poisson smooths gaps and covers unseen outcomes, but doesn't know team strength, form, or injuries - it's a distribution, not a prediction engine
