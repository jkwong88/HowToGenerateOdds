2. How To Generate Final Score Probability Matrix

Every market in this project is priced off one thing: a Home × Away scoreline probability matrix. This section builds that matrix from scratch, from nothing more than a 10-match history.

2.1 Exercise 1 — Historical Match Data

Start from the raw 10-match history (Part 1). Count how often each goal value (0-6) occurred for Home and Away (Part 2), then convert those counts into probabilities (count / 10) (Part 3).

2.2 Exercise 2 — Final Score Probability Matrix

Take the Home and Away goal probabilities from 2.1 as given, and combine them into a joint scoreline matrix: P(Home = h, Away = a) = P(Home = h) × P(Away = a) for every combination of h and a. This matrix is the "given" table every market in section 3 is priced from.

Multiplying the two probabilities together only works if Home and Away goals are independent — for independent events, the probability of both happening is the product of their individual probabilities. That independence is a simplifying modeling assumption this project makes, not something that follows automatically from Home and Away being different teams: in a real match the two sides' scoring can interact (game state, tactics, a team sitting back once ahead, and so on). Treating them as independent ignores that interaction in exchange for a matrix simple enough to build by hand from 10 matches.
