4. Opening Market

A fair market can be built for any line, but a bookmaker only ever lists a few. This section covers how a specific line gets chosen (opened) from everything section 3 makes possible, and how it's priced once it's actually quoted.

4.1 Opening the Market

In this course, we use the line whose two fair selection probabilities are closest to 50% as the candidate main line. This gives us a simple and consistent way to identify a balanced line from the probability model. Real market opening may also consider trading judgment, market information, risk, and other inputs this course doesn't model.

4.2 Adding a Pricing Spread

Take the candidate main line 4.1 selected and shave a pricing spread symmetrically off its fair Malay odds (half off each side). This is how a real, quoted market differs from a fair one: the resulting implied probabilities now sum to slightly more than 100% — an overround — which is how the bookmaker builds in a margin on the quote.
