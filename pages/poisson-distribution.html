5. Relationship between Poisson and Probability

Probability tells us how likely an outcome is. Poisson is one method used to estimate goal probabilities from an expected scoring rate. It does not replace the score-matrix and odds calculations from sections 2–4 — it only changes how the Home/Away goal probabilities that feed into them are produced.

5.1 What We've Been Doing So Far

Sections 2–4 all start from the same rule, applied to the same 10-match history:

Probability of scoring x goals = Count of x goals / Number of matches

5.2 The Limitation of Historical Probability

Home GoalsCountHistorical Probability

Does 0% mean scoring 5 goals is impossible? No — it only means that result didn't occur in this small, 10-match sample.

Historical probability only knows what has already been observed.

With only 10 matches, that can mean:

  • zero probability for outcomes that simply haven't happened yet
  • irregular, noisy-looking distributions
  • exaggerated probabilities built from very few observations

Historical probability asks: “How often did this happen?” Poisson asks: “Given the expected scoring rate, how likely is each possible number of goals?”

5.4 λ — Expected Goals

From the same 10 matches: Home scored goals total, so λ Home = / 10 = . Away scored goals total, so λ Away = / 10 = .

λ represents the expected average number of goals. Poisson then converts it into a full formula:

P(X = k) = e−λ × λk / k!

  • P(X = k) — probability of exactly k goals
  • k — number of goals
  • λ — expected goals
  • e — a mathematical constant (≈ 2.71828)
  • k! — factorial (k × (k−1) × … × 1)

This course doesn't derive or prove that formula — it's about opening odds, not probability theory.

5.7 What Poisson Does and Does Not Do

Poisson helps:

  • smooth the goal probability distribution
  • assign probabilities to unseen outcomes
  • convert expected goals into a complete goal distribution
  • generate a usable score probability matrix

Poisson does not automatically know:

  • team strength
  • opponent strength
  • home advantage
  • injuries
  • recent form
  • tactical matchup
  • red cards or changing match state

Poisson is a probability distribution, not a prediction engine by itself.

Those missing factors are exactly what a more advanced model would fold into λ before Poisson ever runs:

This site stops at Poisson — that bridge to more advanced modeling isn't built here.

Takeaway

Probability tells us how likely an outcome is.

Poisson is one way to estimate that probability using an expected scoring rate.

Once the goal probabilities are produced, the same score-matrix, market, and odds calculations from earlier sections still apply.
